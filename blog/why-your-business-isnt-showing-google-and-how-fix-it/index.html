Why Your Business Isn't Showing Up on Google (and How to Fix It)

You know your business is good. Your customers know it too. So why does someone searching for exactly what you offer end up finding three competitors before they ever see your name? It is one of the most common frustrations we hear at Broadstreet, and the good news is that it is almost always fixable. Here are the usual suspects.

Your Google Business Profile Is Incomplete or Unclaimed

For local search, your Google Business Profile matters even more than your website. It is the box that shows up on Google Maps with your hours, photos, and reviews. If you have never claimed it, or you claimed it years ago and never touched it again, Google has little reason to show you. A complete, active profile with the right categories, accurate hours, and real photos is the single fastest way to start showing up when neighbors search.

You Have Few Reviews, or None at All

Reviews are one of the strongest signals Google uses to decide who to show first. A business with steady, recent reviews looks trustworthy to both Google and the customer scrolling past. If your competitors have fifty reviews and you have three, that gap is doing real damage. The fix is not complicated: you need a simple, consistent way to ask happy customers to leave one, which is something Broadstreet helps local businesses set up.

Your Website Isn't Built for Local Search

A pretty website is not the same as a findable one. If your site never mentions the towns you serve, loads slowly, or is hard to use on a phone, Google struggles to connect you with local searchers. Search engine optimization is what closes that gap, and it is not a one-time task. It is an ongoing process of making your site clearer and more relevant so the right people find you. Our SEO and web development team works on exactly this.

Your Information Isn't Consistent Everywhere

Google cross-checks your name, address, and phone number across the web. If your address is listed one way on Facebook, another way on an old directory, and a third way on your own site, that inconsistency chips away at your credibility in Google's eyes. Cleaning it up so everything matches is tedious but powerful, and it is one of the first things we tackle for new clients.

You Went Quiet

Google favors businesses that stay active. If your profile has no recent posts, your website has not changed in two years, and nothing new is happening online, you look dormant. Regular posts, fresh content, and steady updates tell Google you are open and worth showing. Consistency beats intensity every time.

The Good News: This Is All Fixable

None of these problems require a massive budget or a marketing degree. They just require someone who does this every day.
